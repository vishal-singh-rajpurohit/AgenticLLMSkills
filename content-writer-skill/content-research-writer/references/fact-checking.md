# Fact-Checking & Source Verification

Every factual statement published under a client's or brand's name carries reputational, legal, and search-trust liability. Verify claims systematically before presenting a preview.

## Verification Protocol

For every factual claim in a draft (statistics, pricing, legal/compliance statements, technical limits, dates, benchmarks, competitor comparisons, product specs):

1. **Trace to primary source:** Confirm the source URL directly supports the specific claim—not merely the broad topic.
2. **Verify currency:** Record publication or "last updated" dates. For rapidly changing tech, software pricing, SEO rules, or laws, reject stale multi-year-old blog quotes in favor of current official documentation.
3. **Check conflicting evidence:** When credible sources disagree (e.g., varying implementation benchmarks or regional rules), present the scope/conditions accurately instead of picking one absolute number arbitrarily.
4. **Mark uncertain or unverified items explicitly:** Tag any unconfirmed business claim (e.g., client warranty terms, custom pricing bands, unverified hardware specs, claimed client metrics) with `[NEEDS SME VERIFICATION]` during drafting and list it in [../templates/content-review.md](../templates/content-review.md) before approval.

---

## Strict Non-Fabrication Rules

Never invent, extrapolate, or disguise guesses as facts:
- Customer quotes, testimonials, or named case studies.
- Author credentials, certifications, medical/legal/engineering titles, or awards.
- Survey percentages, ROI numbers, or time-savings claims.
- Hardware/software specifications not verified in official manufacturer/vendor documentation or user-supplied sheets.
- Publication dates or `"verified 2026"` labels for sources not actually checked.

## High-Impact & Regulated Content Gate

For medical, health, legal, financial, tax, compliance, or safety-critical topics (YMYL):
- Require explicit human subject-matter expert (SME) review prior to approval.
- Cite primary government, standards, or official vendor documentation.
- State clear scope/disclaimer context without offering unauthorized professional advice.
